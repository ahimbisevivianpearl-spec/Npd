# Npd
Narcissistic Personality Disorder.

NPD is a personality disorder characterised by a persistent pattern of grandiosity, a strong need for admiration, and difficulty recognising or caring about other people's feelings.

But there's a lot more to it than simply being arrogant or loving yourself.
1. What does someone with NPD behave like?
Some common characteristics include:
- An inflated sense of importance: They believe they're more special, important or deserving than other people.
- Constant need for admiration: They want attention, praise and validation.
- Lack of empathy: They may struggle to understand or consider how their actions affect others.
- Entitlement: They expect special treatment.
- Manipulation: Some may use guilt, blame, or emotional pressure to get what they want.
- Sensitivity to criticism: Even mild criticism can trigger anger, defensiveness or humiliation.
- Exploitation: They may take advantage of people to achieve their own goals.
- Envy: They may feel threatened by other people's success or believe others are jealous of them.
Not everyone with NPD displays all these behaviours.
2. The two common presentations









Grandiose narcissism
- Outwardly confident.
- Seeks admiration and status.
- Acts superior.
- Can be domineering and entitled.












Vulnerable narcissism
- Highly sensitive to rejection.
- Often feels misunderstood.
- May withdraw or play the victim.
- Can be resentful and intensely concerned with how others perceive them.



These are descriptive presentations, not separate official NPD diagnoses.
3. NPD in romantic relationships
This is where things can become particularly complicated.

Some relationship patterns that may occur include:
1. Idealisation: They shower you with attention, affection and compliments.
2. Devaluation: They begin criticising you, withdrawing affection or making you feel inadequate.
3. Blame-shifting: Somehow, every disagreement becomes your fault.
4. Emotional invalidation: Your feelings are dismissed or minimised.
5. Control: They may become uncomfortable when you establish boundaries.
6. Hoovering: After distancing themselves, they may return with affection, promises or apologies to re-establish the relationship.
